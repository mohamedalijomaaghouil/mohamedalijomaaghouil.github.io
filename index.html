CiTIUS · ROBOTA-SUDOE · 2026

The force sensor that read 100× too low

Integrating a 6-axis OnRobot HEX-E v2 force/torque sensor over EtherCAT and publishing it in ROS 2.

×100measurement error traced to byte decoding
2.77 → 0.69 Nmean error after calibration

Calibration, validated on an independent mass

The calibration was fitted on 625 g data only, then tested on a 1051 g mass it had never seen, without refitting. Mean error 2.77 N → 0.69 N.

Error per pose (table)
PoseRaw (N)Calibrated (N)

The story

  • The sensor's readings were about 100 times too small. Instead of patching the numbers, I went down to the raw EtherCAT bytes and found the decoding error.
  • Fixed the driver, then built a 3×3 calibration.
  • Validated it on an independent dataset: mean error dropped from 2.77 N to 0.69 N.

SIGMA Clermont · EU project REMAIN-SUDOE · 2025–26

Robotic defect inspection with a UR3e

Semester 7–8 project of 8 students, ranked 2nd of 93; I was in the robotics sub-team of 3. A UR3e with an Intel RealSense camera photographs a shoe from several angles to detect defects.

My part: where is the shoe?

My localization node live on the real RealSense camera (13 March 2026): as the shoe is moved and turned, the log updates its pixel position, distance and yaw.

How the node works

  1. Table depthmedian of the bottom of the depth image
  2. Segmentkeep pixels at least 3 cm closer than the table
  3. Cleanmorphological opening; reject blobs too small, too large or touching the border
  4. Pose in the imagelargest valid contour → minimum-area rectangle → centre (u, v) and yaw
  5. To 3Dmedian depth around the centre, pinhole back-projection → X, Y, Z in metres
  6. PublishJSON on /shoe_pose in ROS 2, or present: false if nothing is found
  • A live annotated control image (bounding box, distance, orientation) to follow the inspection.
  • Tested on a ROS 2 recording (285 synchronised colour + depth frames), live in Gazebo, then on the real camera above the UR3e. With no shoe on the table, the node reports "no object" instead of a false position.

My two robotics teammates handled motion planning and the Gazebo simulation; the other students built the dataset and the deep-learning defect detection.

ENISo · 2nd-year project · Jan – May 2025

FOLLOW-ME: a person-following robot

Team of 3. A mobile robot that detects a person, starts following on a raised hand, and keeps a safe distance.

2 m ± 0.1following distance
~20 fpsprocessing rate

How it works

  • MediaPipe finds the shoulders; their midpoint gives the torso position. An Intel RealSense D435i gives its distance.
  • A hand gesture starts and stops the tracking; the robot stops safely if the person leaves the field of view.
  • Linear and angular speeds (Vx, Wz) are sent to the base over Modbus TCP.
